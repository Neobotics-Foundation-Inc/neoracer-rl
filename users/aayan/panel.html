The five methods

How a continuous problem became a table

The three state features

lateral — left beam minus right beam. Am I off-centre?
skew — rear-side minus front-side beam, left minus right. Am I pointed along the wall or into it?
forward — distance to the wall ahead. Is a corner coming?

Without skew the state cannot tell running parallel to a wall from driving into it, and an agent scores below a random policy. It roughly doubles lap fraction.

Two things that mattered more than any algorithm

Torque. The drive motors take ctrlrange="-1 1" in N·m and 0.35 is enough to flip the car at speed. An action grid reaching 1.0 only teaches a policy to drive into the nearest wall, so the three settings here stay in the cruising range: 0.05, 0.10 and 0.18.

Wall contact ends the episode. It used to cost a penalty for every step the car spent scraping, which made standing still the best policy and needed the reward rebalanced before anything would learn. The environment now ends the episode on contact with a one-time crash penalty equal to the stuck penalty, so an episode that makes progress and then crashes already returns more than one that never moves. Every method here trains on the environment's own reward.

Why generated tracks cannot be trained on

The environment raises a ValueError for any track but loop_corridor.xml. Its reward, lap detection and spawn logic are all written around that ring's geometry — progress is measured as angle swept about the ring's centre, which means nothing on a free-form loop. Driving a generated track works fine; training on one needs a progress metric that follows a centreline.
